# Devices Tycoon research (for Silicon Behemoths)

Caveat: the closest match found is "Devices Company Tycoon Inc" (ArraGames, Steam Early Access, 27 Mar 2026, cheap). There is also a separate "Devices Tycoon" by Roastery Games (moddb.com/games/devices-tycoon) with no reviews. No Reddit, YouTube or patch-note coverage was found, so UI/UX and complaint sections are thin and partly inferred from the store rating and dev statements. Sources: store.steampowered.com/app/2257920, arragames.itch.io/devices-company-tycoon-inc, gamegeeker.com/games/computer-tycoon, nodal.gg/game/3248690.

## 1. Core loop and systems
- Design: detailed device editor (size, colour, screen, CPU/GPU, speakers, packaging); itch page claims 10,000+ options. Many device types: phones, tablets, laptops, watches, headphones, TVs, PCs. (steam, itch)
- Components/OS: tune processors, build custom processors, create your own OS. (steam)
- Staff/offices: hire designers, programmers, engineers; upgrade and expand offices, open global offices. (steam, itch)
- Marketing/launch: product presentations, marketing campaigns, simulated early-adopter reviews that feed back into the design and ratings. (steam, itch)
- Retail: open stores worldwide, negotiate deals. (steam)
- Rivals: competitor rankings, acquire rival companies, leaderboards, contracts. (steam)
- Market: trends shift; random events affect success. (steam, itch)
- Start: choose company name and starting capital. Modding for devices, components, scenarios. (itch, steam)
- Not documented: eras/start year, research tree, manufacturing lines.

## 2. UI/UX
- Not documented in detail. Dev says balance, progression pacing and UI/UX are the focus areas before 1.0 (steam), implying these are the rough spots.
- Rating is Mixed (about 53-58% of 12-14 reviews), so no strong praise signal; the editor depth and low price are the selling points.

## 3. Top 15 ideas to copy (by player value)
1. Early-adopter/reviewer feedback after a prototype, telling you what to fix before launch.
2. Deep device editor with visible stat consequences per choice.
3. Multiple device categories sharing one design flow.
4. Custom processor/chip design as its own sub-project.
5. Build-your-own OS as a long-term asset.
6. Product presentation events with a prep-vs-payoff choice.
7. Marketing campaigns with targeted regions or segments.
8. Retail store network with negotiated deals.
9. Rival rankings visible at a glance, plus leaderboard.
10. Acquiring rival companies as a late-game goal.
11. Contracts as side income and early-game bootstrap.
12. Starting-capital and company-name setup for replayability.
13. Role-based hiring (designer, programmer, engineer) with distinct contributions.
14. Office upgrades and global offices as expansion milestones.
15. Random events and shifting market trends to force adaptation; scenario/mod support.

## 4. Complaints to avoid
- Mixed reviews with unclear causes; dev-flagged problem areas: unbalanced economy, slow or uneven progression, rough UI/UX.
- Early Access feel: thin content at launch, so keep the core loop finished and polished.
- Name confusion with similar titles; keep branding distinct.
